Graphing in Vertex Form

College Algebra  ·  §4.3  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  STUDENT

A parabola has one most important point, its turning point. If $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$, which two numbers in the equation tell you exactly where that point is, and what single number tells you whether it is a peak or a valley?

Vertex Form $$f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k,\quad a\neq0$$ The graph is a parabola with vertex $(h,k)$ and axis of symmetry $x=h$. Nothing needs to be calculated: read $h$ and $k$ straight from the equation.

Reading the Form

$a>0$ → opens up; the vertex is the minimum value $k$
$a<0$ → opens down; the vertex is the maximum value $k$
Larger $|a|$ → narrower parabola

Watch the sign of $h$: $(x+2)^2=(x-(-2))^2$, so $h=-2$. The sign of $k$ is just as it appears.

$f(x)=2(x-3)^2+1$: vertex $(3,1)$, up
$f(x)=-(x+2)^2-4$: vertex $(-2,-4)$, down

Graphing Recipe
  1. Read the vertex $(h,k)$ and plot it.
  2. Check the sign of $a$: up or down.
  3. Sketch the curve from the vertex, symmetric about $x=h$.

y-intercept: set $x=0$: $f(0)=ah^2+k$.

Example: $f(x)=(x-3)^2-1$: $f(0)=9-1=8$, so $(0,8)$.

▸ Show Proof

Since $(x-h)^2\ge0$ for every $x$, we have $a(x-h)^2$ has the sign of $a$ (or is $0$).

If $a>0$: $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k\ge k$, with equality only at $x=h$. So $(h,k)$ is the lowest point.

If $a<0$: $f(x)\le k$, with equality only at $x=h$. So $(h,k)$ is the highest point.

Symmetry: $f(h+t)=at^2+k=f(h-t)$, so points equally far left and right of $x=h$ have equal heights. The line $x=h$ is the axis of symmetry.

∎

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Read the vertex
State the vertex and direction of $f(x)=2(x-3)^2+1$.
$h=3$, $k=1$, $a=2>0$
Vertex $(3,1)$, opens up (minimum value $1$)
Example 2  —  Watch the sign and graph it
Graph $g(x)=-(x+2)^2+3$.
$x+2=x-(-2)$, so $h=-2$, $k=3$. $a=-1<0$: opens down.
Vertex $(-2,3)$, maximum value $3$.
Example 3  —  y-intercept
Find the $y$-intercept of $f(x)=(x-3)^2-1$.
$f(0)=(0-3)^2-1=8$
$y$-intercept: $(0,8)$
Example 4  —  Find $a$
A parabola has vertex $(1,-2)$ and passes through $(3,2)$. Write its equation in vertex form.
$f(x)=a(x-1)^2-2$. Plug in $(3,2)$: $2=a(2)^2-2\Rightarrow 4a=4\Rightarrow a=1$
$f(x)=(x-1)^2-2$

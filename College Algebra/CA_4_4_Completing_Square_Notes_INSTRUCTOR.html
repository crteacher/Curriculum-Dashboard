Completing the Square

College Algebra  ·  §4.4  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

Vertex form shows the turning point and factored form shows the zeros, but quadratics often arrive as $x^2+6x+5$. Is there a method that turns standard form into both of the other forms? And if you ran that method on $ax^2+bx+c$ with letters instead of numbers, what would you get?
Standard to Vertex Form

Key identity: $x^2+bx=\left(x+\dfrac b2\right)^2-\left(\dfrac b2\right)^2$

  1. If $a\neq1$, factor $a$ out of the $x^2$ and $x$ terms.
  2. Take half the $x$-coefficient and square it.
  3. Add and subtract it inside the parentheses; the first three terms become a perfect square.
  4. Collect the constants: this is $a(x-h)^2+k$.

$x^2+6x+5=(x+3)^2-9+5=(x+3)^2-4$

▸ Show Proof

Expand the right side of the identity:

$$\left(x+\frac b2\right)^2-\left(\frac b2\right)^2=x^2+bx+\frac{b^2}{4}-\frac{b^2}{4}=x^2+bx.$$

The $\frac{b^2}{4}$ terms cancel, so adding and subtracting the square of half the coefficient does not change the value.

∎

Vertex to Factored Form

The zeros are where $f(x)=0$:

  1. Set $a(x-h)^2+k=0$.
  2. Isolate the square: $(x-h)^2=-\dfrac ka$.
  3. Take square roots: $x=h\pm\sqrt{-k/a}$.
  4. Write $f(x)=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$.

$(x+3)^2-4=0\Rightarrow x+3=\pm2\Rightarrow x=-1,\,-5$
So $x^2+6x+5=(x+1)(x+5)$.

If $-k/a<0$ there are no real zeros; if it is not a perfect square the zeros are irrational and factoring would not have found them.

The Same Steps on $ax^2+bx+c$: the Quadratic Formula

Do exactly those steps with letters. The result is $\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\dfrac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}$, which gives the formula you will use next section.

▸ Show Proof

Solve $ax^2+bx+c=0$ by completing the square.

Divide by $a$ and move the constant: $$x^2+\frac ba\,x=-\frac ca.$$

Add $\left(\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2$ to both sides: $$x^2+\frac ba\,x+\frac{b^2}{4a^2}=\frac{b^2}{4a^2}-\frac ca=\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}.$$

The left side is a perfect square: $$\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}.$$

Take square roots: $$x+\frac{b}{2a}=\pm\frac{\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\quad\Rightarrow\quad x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}.$$

∎

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Standard to vertex form
Write $f(x)=x^2+6x+5$ in vertex form and find the vertex.
Half of $6$ is $3$; $3^2=9$.
$f(x)=(x+3)^2-9+5=(x+3)^2-4$. Vertex $(-3,-4)$.
Example 2  —  Vertex to factored form
Use $f(x)=(x+3)^2-4$ to find the zeros and write $f$ in factored form.
$(x+3)^2=4\Rightarrow x+3=\pm2\Rightarrow x=-1,\,-5$
$f(x)=(x+1)(x+5)$. Check: $x^2+6x+5$. ✓
Example 3  —  When $a\neq1$
For $f(x)=2x^2-8x-10$ find vertex form, zeros, and factored form.
$f=2(x^2-4x)-10=2\big[(x-2)^2-4\big]-10=2(x-2)^2-18$
$2(x-2)^2=18\Rightarrow x-2=\pm3\Rightarrow x=5,\,-1$
$f(x)=2(x-5)(x+1)$
Example 4  —  Use the proof's result
Solve $x^2+2x-1=0$ using $\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}$.
$a=1,b=2,c=-1$: $\dfrac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}=\dfrac{8}{4}=2$
$(x+1)^2=2\Rightarrow x=-1\pm\sqrt2$ (irrational, so factoring would not work)
Practice Problems
1.
Write $f(x)=x^2+10x+9$ in vertex form.
$(x+5)^2-16$
2.
For $f(x)=x^2-6x+5$ give the vertex form and the zeros.
$(x-3)^2-4$; zeros $x=1,\,5$
3.
For $f(x)=2x^2+12x+10$ give the vertex and factored form.
$2(x+3)^2-8$, vertex $(-3,-8)$; zeros $-1,-5$; $f=2(x+1)(x+5)$
4.
For $f(x)=-x^2+4x+5$ give vertex form and factored form.
$-(x-2)^2+9$; zeros $5,-1$; $f=-(x-5)(x+1)$
5.
Solve $x^2+4x-1=0$ by completing the square.
$(x+2)^2=5\Rightarrow x=-2\pm\sqrt5$
6.
Solve $2x^2+4x-6=0$ using $\left(x+\frac{b}{2a}\right)^2=\frac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}$.
$\dfrac{16+48}{16}=4$, so $(x+1)^2=4\Rightarrow x=1,\,-3$

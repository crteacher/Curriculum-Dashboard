Slope-Intercept and Point-Slope Forms

College Algebra  ·  §2.2  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

A line passes through $(3, 7)$ with slope 2. How do you write the equation of this line? What information do you need, and which form uses it most directly?
Slope-Intercept Form

$y = mx + b$
$m$ = slope    $b$ = y-intercept

Use when you know slope and y-intercept.
e.g. $m = -3$, $b = 5 \Rightarrow y = -3x + 5$

Converting standard form:
Solve for $y$.
e.g. $6x - 3y = 9$:
$-3y = -6x + 9 \Rightarrow y = 2x - 3$

▸ Show Proof

A line with slope $m$ and y-intercept $b$ passes through $(0, b)$. For any other point $(x, y)$ on the line, the slope formula gives:

$$m = \frac{y - b}{x - 0} = \frac{y - b}{x}$$

Multiply both sides by $x$: $mx = y - b$. Add $b$: $y = mx + b$.

Slope-intercept form is what you get when you plug the y-intercept point into the slope formula and solve for $y$.

∎

Slope-intercept form is best for graphing and identifying slope quickly.

Point-Slope Form

$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$
$(x_1, y_1)$ = known point on the line

Use when you know slope and a point.
e.g. $m = 4$, point $(1, 3)$:
$y - 3 = 4(x - 1) \Rightarrow y = 4x - 1$

Two points given:
Find slope first, then apply point-slope.
e.g. $(2,1)$ and $(4,7)$:
$m = \frac{7-1}{4-2} = 3$, then $y-1=3(x-2) \Rightarrow y=3x-5$

▸ Show Proof

Start from the slope formula with known point $(x_1, y_1)$ and arbitrary point $(x, y)$ on the line:

$$m = \frac{y - y_1}{x - x_1}$$

Multiply both sides by $(x - x_1)$:

$$m(x - x_1) = y - y_1$$

That is point-slope form — it is the slope definition rearranged, nothing more.

∎

Graphing a Line

From slope-intercept form $y = mx + b$:

  1. Plot the y-intercept $(0, b)$.
  2. Write the slope as a fraction $m = \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}$. From the intercept, move run units horizontally and rise units vertically to locate a second point.
  3. Draw a straight line through the two points, extending in both directions.

e.g. for $y = 2x - 1$: start at $(0, -1)$. Slope $2 = \dfrac{2}{1}$, so move right 1, up 2, to reach $(1, 1)$. Draw the line through both points.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Slope-intercept from m and b
Write the equation of a line with $m = -3$ and $b = 5$.
$y = -3x + 5$
y = -3x + 5, plotted from the y-intercept and slope
Example 2  —  Convert from standard form
Convert $6x - 3y = 9$ to slope-intercept form.
$-3y = -6x + 9 \Rightarrow y = 2x - 3$
Example 3  —  Point-slope form
Write the equation of the line with $m = 4$ through $(1, 3)$.
$y - 3 = 4(x - 1) \Rightarrow y = 4x - 1$
y = 4x - 1, plotted from the given point and slope
Example 4  —  Line through two points
Write the equation of the line through $(2, 1)$ and $(4, 7)$.
$m = \frac{6}{2} = 3$; $y - 1 = 3(x-2) \Rightarrow y = 3x - 5$

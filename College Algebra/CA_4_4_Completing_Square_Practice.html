Completing the Square

College Algebra  ·  §4.4  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
Standard → Vertex Form Complete the square: $x^2+bx = \left(x+\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\dfrac{b}{2}\right)^{2}$. So $x^2+bx+c=\left(x+\dfrac b2\right)^2+\left(c-\dfrac{b^2}{4}\right)$, giving the vertex $\left(-\dfrac b2,\;c-\dfrac{b^2}{4}\right)$. If $a\neq1$, factor $a$ out of the $x^2$ and $x$ terms first.
Vertex → Factored Form Set $a(x-h)^2+k=0$, isolate the square, and take square roots: $x=h\pm\sqrt{-k/a}$. The roots are the zeros, so $f(x)=a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$.
Same Steps on ax² + bx + c Do exactly these steps with letters and you get $\left(x+\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^{2}=\dfrac{b^2-4ac}{4a^2}$, which is the quadratic formula. No tricks.

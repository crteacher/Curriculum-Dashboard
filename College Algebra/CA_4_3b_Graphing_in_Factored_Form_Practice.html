Graphing in Factored Form

College Algebra  ·  §4.3b  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
Factored Form f(x) = a(x − r₁)(x − r₂) The x-intercepts (zeros) are $x=r_1$ and $x=r_2$. Watch the signs: $(x+3)$ gives the zero $x=-3$.
Getting to the vertex The parabola is symmetric, so the vertex sits exactly halfway between the zeros: $h=\dfrac{r_1+r_2}{2}$. Then find $k=f(h)$ by plugging $h$ in. The vertex form is $f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k$.
Practice Problems

Typed problems: integers, a pair like -3,5 (smaller first), or a vertex form like (x-1)^2-4 (write -(x-1)^2+4 when it opens down). Press Check or hit Enter. Press Check or hit Enter. Graph problems: use the ● x-intercept tool to mark both zeros, the Vertex tool to mark the vertex, then choose Opens up or Opens down. Click a mark again to remove it.

Solving Quadratic Equations

College Algebra  ·  §4.2  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
1 · Solve directly (square roots) No $x$ term? Isolate the square, then take $\pm\sqrt{\ }$ of both sides: $x^2=k \Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt{k}$. Works for $(x-h)^2=k$ too.
2 · Already factored (Zero Product Property) If $A \cdot B = 0$ then $A=0$ or $B=0$. Set each factor equal to $0$ and solve each one.
3 · Not factored? Factor it With $=0$ on one side, factor: GCF first, then $x^2+bx+c=(x+p)(x+q)$ where $pq=c$ and $p+q=b$, or a difference of squares $x^2-k^2=(x+k)(x-k)$. Then use the Zero Product Property.
4 · Not equal to zero? Rearrange first The Zero Product Property only works when one side is $0$. Move every term to one side, then factor. Example: $(x+1)(x-4)=6$ must be multiplied out and rearranged before factoring.

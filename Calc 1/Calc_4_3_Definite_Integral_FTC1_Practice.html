The Definite Integral

Calculus I  ·  §4.3  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
The Definite Integral $$\int_a^b f(x)\,dx = \lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f(x_i)\,\Delta x$$ It is the signed area between $f$ and the x-axis: area above counts positive, area below counts negative. $\sum i = \tfrac{n(n+1)}{2}$    $\sum i^2 = \tfrac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$
Properties $\int_a^a f=0$;  $\int_a^b f=-\int_b^a f$;  $\int_a^b (cf\pm g)=c\int_a^b f\pm\int_a^b g$;  $\int_a^b f=\int_a^c f+\int_c^b f$.

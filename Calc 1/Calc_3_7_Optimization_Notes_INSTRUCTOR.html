Optimization

Calculus I  ·  §3.7  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

A garden store wants to build a rectangular pen using 120 feet of fencing, using the side of their building as one wall (no fence needed there). What's the largest area they can enclose — and is "fence around every side equally" actually the best strategy?
Optimization Strategy
  1. Identify what's being maximized or minimized. Write it as a function of one variable.
  2. Find a constraint equation relating the variables; solve for one variable and substitute it in.
  3. Find critical points: set the derivative equal to $0$ (or find where it's undefined), and solve.
  4. Check the endpoints of the domain too, and compare all candidates to find the actual max/min.
Identifying the Domain

Real-world constraints often make the domain a closed interval, smaller than "all real numbers." Lengths must be positive, cut squares can't exceed half the shorter side, and so on.

Always ask: do the endpoints make physical sense? A critical point that falls outside the valid domain is not a candidate at all.

▸ Common Mistake

Finding a critical point and assuming it's automatically the answer, without checking (a) that it's actually inside the valid domain, and (b) that it beats the endpoints and any other critical points.

On a closed interval, the max/min is the largest/smallest value among: all critical points inside the domain, and both endpoints.

▸ Why Check Endpoints? A Cautionary Example

For the box-from-cardboard problem below (Example 1), solving $V'(s)=0$ actually gives two solutions: $s=3$ and $s=\tfrac{35}{3}\approx11.67$.

But the valid domain is $(0,7)$ — cutting more than $7$ inches from the $14$-inch side is physically impossible. So $s=\tfrac{35}{3}$ is thrown out immediately; it's not a real candidate, even though it solves the equation.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Box from cardboard
A $14\times30$ in sheet of cardboard has equal squares of side $s$ cut from each corner; the sides fold up into an open-top box. What size squares maximize the volume?
$V(s)=s(14-2s)(30-2s)$, domain $(0,7)$.
Expand: $V=4s^3-88s^2+420s$; $V'=12s^2-176s+420=4(3s^2-44s+105)$
$3s^2-44s+105=0\Rightarrow s=\dfrac{44\pm\sqrt{676}}{6}=\dfrac{44\pm26}{6}\Rightarrow s=3\text{ or }s=\tfrac{35}{3}$
Only $s=3$ is in $(0,7)$. $V(3)=3(8)(24)=576\text{ in}^3$ (max)
Example 2  —  Closest point on a line
Which point on the graph of $y=x$ is closest to the point $(7,0)$?
Minimize $D^2=(x-7)^2+x^2$ (dropping the square root simplifies the algebra).
$\dfrac{d}{dx}[D^2]=2(x-7)+2x=4x-14=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac{7}{2}$
Point: $\left(\tfrac{7}{2},\tfrac{7}{2}\right)$; minimum distance $=\tfrac{7\sqrt2}{2}$
Example 3  —  Fencing a yard
Using $120$ ft of fencing and a building wall as one side, what dimensions maximize the enclosed rectangular area?
Let $x=$ side parallel to the wall, $y=$ each perpendicular side: $x+2y=120\Rightarrow x=120-2y$
$A(y)=xy=(120-2y)y=120y-2y^2$
$A'(y)=120-4y=0\Rightarrow y=30,\;x=60$
Maximum area $=60(30)=1800\text{ ft}^2$
Example 4  —  Minimizing a poster's area
A poster needs $96\text{ in}^2$ of printed area, with $2$-in margins on each side and $3$-in margins top and bottom. What overall dimensions minimize the poster's total area?
Printed: $x$ by $\tfrac{96}{x}$. Total area $S(x)=(x+4)\left(\tfrac{96}{x}+6\right)=6x+\tfrac{384}{x}+120$
$S'(x)=6-\tfrac{384}{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x^2=64\Rightarrow x=8,\;y=\tfrac{96}{8}=12$
Total dimensions: $12\times18$ in; minimum total area $=216\text{ in}^2$
Practice Problems
1.
Two positive numbers have a sum of $20$. Maximize their product.
$P(x)=x(20-x)$, $P'=20-2x=0\Rightarrow x=10$. Max product $=100$
2.
An open-top box is made from a $12\times12$ in square by cutting corner squares of side $x$. Maximize the volume. (What's the domain?)
$V(x)=x(12-2x)^2$, domain $(0,6)$. $V'=(12-2x)(12-6x)=0\Rightarrow x=2$ (interior) or $x=6$ (endpoint, $V=0$). $V(2)=2(8)^2=128\text{ in}^3$
3.
A box with a square base and volume $32\text{ ft}^3$ has an open top. Minimize its surface area.
$S(x)=x^2+\tfrac{128}{x}$ (since $h=32/x^2$). $S'=2x-\tfrac{128}{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x^3=64\Rightarrow x=4,h=2$. Min $S=16+32=48\text{ ft}^2$
4.
Two adjacent rectangular pens share one middle divider; $240$ ft of fencing total (2 lengths + 3 widths). Maximize the total enclosed area.
$2x+3y=240\Rightarrow x=\tfrac{240-3y}{2}$. $A(y)=xy$, $A'=0\Rightarrow y=40,\;x=60$. Max area $=2400\text{ ft}^2$
5.
An open-top box with a square base and volume $27\text{ ft}^3$ costs \$2/ft² for the base and \$1/ft² for the sides. Minimize the total cost.
$C(x)=2x^2+\tfrac{108}{x}$ (since $h=27/x^2$). $C'=4x-\tfrac{108}{x^2}=0\Rightarrow x^3=27\Rightarrow x=3,h=3$. Min cost $=18+36=\$54$
6.
A rectangle is inscribed in a semicircle of radius $5$, with its base on the diameter. Maximize its area.
Half-base $x$, height $\sqrt{25-x^2}$: $A(x)=2x\sqrt{25-x^2}$. $A'=0\Rightarrow x^2=\tfrac{25}{2}\Rightarrow x=\tfrac{5\sqrt2}{2}$. Max area $=25$

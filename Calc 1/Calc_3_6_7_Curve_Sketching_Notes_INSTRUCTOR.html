Curve Sketching

Calculus I  ·  §3.6.7  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

You can find zeros, classify critical points, and determine concavity. How do you assemble all of that into a single, complete picture of a function's graph?
The Curve-Sketching Checklist

Work through these steps in order:

  1. Domain — any restrictions?
  2. Intercepts — $y$-int: $f(0)$; $x$-int: solve $f(x)=0$
  3. Critical numbers — $f'(x)=0$ or DNE; evaluate $f$
  4. Increasing / Decreasing — sign of $f'$ on each interval
  5. Inflection points — $f''(x)=0$ with sign change
  6. Concavity — sign of $f''$ on each interval
  7. End behavior — $f(x)$ as $x\to\pm\infty$

No new calculus here — this organizes tools from §§3.5–3.6.5 into a coherent picture. A sketch doesn’t need to be exact; it needs to be honest: correct turns, right concavity, accurate end behavior.

Worked Example
Example 1  —  Complete sketch — $f(x)=x^3-3x$
Work through all seven checklist steps. Produce a labeled sketch.
1. Domain: all reals.
2. Intercepts: $f(0)=0$; $x(x^2-3)=0\Rightarrow x=0,\,\pm\sqrt{3}\approx\pm1.73$.
3. Critical numbers: $f'(x)=3x^2-3=3(x-1)(x+1)=0\Rightarrow x=\pm1$.
 $f(-1)=2$ — local max;  $f(1)=-2$ — local min.
4. Inc/Dec: $f'>0$ on $(-\infty,-1)$ and $(1,\infty)$; $f'<0$ on $(-1,1)$.
5–6. Inflection & Concavity: $f''(x)=6x=0\Rightarrow x=0$. Sign changes: concave down on $(-\infty,0)$, concave up on $(0,\infty)$. Inflection at $(0,0)$.
7. End behavior: $x^3$ dominates — $f\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$, $f\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$.
$f(x)=x^3-3x$: zeros, local max/min, inflection, and end behavior all labeled
Practice Problems
1.
Find and classify all critical numbers of $f(x)=x^3-12x+4$.
$f'=3x^2-12=0\Rightarrow x=\pm2$. $f''(-2)<0$: local max at $(-2,20)$. $f''(2)>0$: local min at $(2,-12)$.
2.
Find the inflection point of $f(x)=x^3-12x+4$ and the concavity on each side.
$f''=6x=0$ at $x=0$; changes sign. Inflection $(0,4)$. CD on $(-\infty,0)$; CU on $(0,\infty)$.
3.
State the end behavior of $f(x)=x^3-12x+4$.
$x^3$ dominates: $f\to-\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$; $f\to+\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$.
4.
For $f(x)=-x^3+3x^2$, find all critical numbers and classify each.
$f'=-3x^2+6x=-3x(x-2)=0$ at $x=0,2$. $f''(0)=6>0$: local min $(0,0)$. $f''(2)=-6<0$: local max $(2,4)$.
5.
For $f(x)=-x^3+3x^2$, find the inflection point and describe end behavior.
$f''=-6x+6=0$ at $x=1$: inflection $(1,2)$. End: $+\infty$ as $x\to-\infty$; $-\infty$ as $x\to+\infty$.
6.
Without computing zeros exactly, describe the shape of $f(x)=x^4-4x^2$: how many turning points, what end behavior, where does it dip?
$f'=4x^3-8x=4x(x^2-2)=0$ at $x=0,\pm\sqrt{2}$. 3 critical points (2 mins, 1 max at origin). $f''=12x^2-8$: 2 inflection points at $x=\pm1/\sqrt{3}$. Both ends go $+\infty$ (even degree, positive leading coeff).

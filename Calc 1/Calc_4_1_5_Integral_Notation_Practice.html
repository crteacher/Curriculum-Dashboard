Integral Notation & Geometric Area

Calculus I  ·  §4.1.5  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
Geometric Rules (no antiderivatives needed) $\displaystyle\int_a^b c\,f\,dx = c\int_a^b f\,dx$   $\displaystyle\int_a^b(f\pm g)\,dx = \int_a^b f\,dx \pm \int_a^b g\,dx$   $\displaystyle\int_a^b f\,dx + \int_b^c f\,dx = \int_a^c f\,dx$
Signed Area & Flipped Limits Area below the $x$-axis is negative. Flipping the limits negates the integral: $\displaystyle\int_b^a f\,dx = -\int_a^b f\,dx$. If a region is symmetric about the $y$-axis and the function is odd ($f(-x)=-f(x)$), the positive and negative pieces cancel: $\displaystyle\int_{-a}^{a} f\,dx = 0$.
Shapes to Know Rectangle: $\displaystyle\int_a^b c\,dx = c(b-a)$   Right triangle: $\displaystyle\int_0^b x\,dx = \tfrac{1}{2}b^2$   Trapezoid: average height × base width.

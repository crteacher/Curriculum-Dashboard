Sketching the Antiderivative

Calculus I  ·  §4.5.5  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

You've seen how $f'$ tells you the slope of $f$ at every point. Now flip the question: if someone hands you $f$ — the slope function at every $x$ — can you recover a function $F$ whose slope matches it? And what does that recovered curve actually look like?
Reading F from f

If $F' = f$, then $F$ is an antiderivative of $f$. To sketch $F$ from the graph of $f$:

  • $f(x) > 0$ → $F$ is increasing
  • $f(x) < 0$ → $F$ is decreasing
  • $f(x) = 0$ → $F$ has a local max or min
  • $f$ large positive → $F$ rises steeply
  • $f$ large negative → $F$ falls steeply

The height of $f$ at $x$ gives the slope of $F$ at $x$.

This is the mirror image of §2.2.7: there, the height of $f'$ gave the slope of $f$. Here, the height of $f$ gives the slope of $F$. The logic is identical — only the direction changes.

The Full Chain: $f'$, $f$, $F$

$F \xrightarrow{\text{differentiate}} f \xrightarrow{\text{differentiate}} f'$
$F \xleftarrow{\text{integrate}} f \xleftarrow{\text{integrate}} f'$

Moving right: slope of left = height of right.
Moving left: accumulated area under right = change in left.

Example: $f(x)=x^2$ ⇒ $f'(x)=2x$  and  $F(x)=\frac{x^3}{3}+C$

The $+C$ family: There are infinitely many antiderivatives — all vertical shifts of each other. When sketching $F$ from a graph, choose a convenient starting height (e.g. $F(0)=0$) unless told otherwise.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Constant $f$ → Linear $F$
The left panel shows $f(x) = 0.6$. Describe and sketch $F$.
$f > 0$ everywhere, so $F$ is always increasing at constant rate $0.6$.
No zeros ⇒ no critical points of $F$.
Antiderivative: $F(x) = 0.6x + C$ — a straight line with slope $0.6$.
Key takeaway: constant $f$ gives linear $F$; the value of $f$ is the slope of $F$.
Example 2  —  Linear $f$ → Quadratic $F$
The left panel shows $f(x) = -\frac{x}{2}$. Where does $F$ have a critical point, and is it a max or min?
$f = 0$ at $x = 0$, so $F$ has a critical point at $x = 0$.
For $x < 0$: $f > 0$ ⇒ $F$ increasing. For $x > 0$: $f < 0$ ⇒ $F$ decreasing.
Increasing then decreasing ⇒ $x = 0$ is a local maximum of $F$.
Antiderivative: $F(x) = -\dfrac{x^2}{4} + C$.
Example 3  —  Parabola $f$ → Cubic $F$
The left panel shows $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}(x^2 - 1)$. Find the critical points of $F$ and classify them.
$f = 0$ at $x = \pm 1$, so $F$ has critical points at $x = -1$ and $x = 1$.
$f < 0$ on $(-1, 1)$ ⇒ $F$ decreasing there.
$f > 0$ outside $(-1, 1)$ ⇒ $F$ increasing outside.
Therefore: local max at $x = -1$, local min at $x = 1$.
Antiderivative: $F(x) = \frac{1}{2}\!\left(\frac{x^3}{3} - x\right) + C$.
Example 4  —  Reading in reverse: given $f'$, sketch $f$
The left panel shows $f'(x) = \frac{x}{2}$. Locate the critical point of $f$ and sketch $f$.
$f' = 0$ at $x = 0$ ⇒ $f$ has a critical point at $x = 0$.
$f' < 0$ for $x < 0$ ⇒ $f$ decreasing. $f' > 0$ for $x > 0$ ⇒ $f$ increasing.
Decreasing then increasing ⇒ $x = 0$ is a local minimum of $f$.
Antiderivative of $f'$: $f(x) = \frac{x^2}{4} + C$ — an upward parabola.
This is exactly §2.2.7 in reverse: there you read $f'$ from $f$; here you read $f$ from $f'$.
Practice Problems
1.
Describe $F$ if $f(x) = -0.8$ (constant negative).
$f < 0$ everywhere ⇒ $F$ always decreasing. No zeros ⇒ no critical points. $F(x) = -0.8x + C$.
2.
$f(x) = \frac{x}{3}$. Where is the critical point of $F$? Is it a max or min?
$f = 0$ at $x = 0$. $f < 0$ for $x < 0$ (decreasing), $f > 0$ for $x > 0$ (increasing). ⇒ local minimum of $F$ at $x = 0$. $F(x) = \frac{x^2}{6} + C$.
3.
$f(x) = -(x^2 - 4)$. Find the critical points of $F$ and classify them.
$f = 0$ at $x = \pm 2$. $f < 0$ outside $(-2,2)$; $f > 0$ inside. ⇒ $F$ has local min at $x = -2$ and local max at $x = 2$.
4.
$f'(x) = -x$. Find the critical point of $f$ and sketch its shape.
$f' = 0$ at $x = 0$. $f' > 0$ for $x < 0$ (increasing); $f' < 0$ for $x > 0$ (decreasing). ⇒ local maximum of $f$ at $x = 0$. $f(x) = -\frac{x^2}{2} + C$ (down parabola).
5.
If $F$ has a local minimum at $x = 2$, what must $f(2)$ equal?
$F' = f$, so $F' = 0$ at a critical point. ⇒ $f(2) = 0$.
6.
$f'(x) = 3$ (constant). What is $f$? What is $F$ (antiderivative of $f$)?
$f(x) = 3x + C_1$ (linear). $F(x) = \frac{3x^2}{2} + C_1 x + C_2$ (quadratic).

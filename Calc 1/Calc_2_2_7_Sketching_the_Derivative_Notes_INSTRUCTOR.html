Sketching the Derivative

Calculus I  ·  §2.2.7  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

The graph of $f$ is shown below. Without calculating anything, where do you think $f'(x) = 0$? Where is $f'$ positive? Negative?

▸ Think about it, then reveal
$f'(x) = 0$ at the local max ($x = -1$) and local min ($x = 1$) — these are where the tangent is horizontal.
$f'(x) > 0$ where $f$ is increasing (to the left of $x=-1$ and right of $x=1$).
$f'(x) < 0$ where $f$ is decreasing (between $x=-1$ and $x=1$).
Reading f′ from the Graph of f

The key idea: the height of the $f'$ graph at $x$ equals the slope of the $f$ graph at $x$.

  • $f$ increasing on $(a,b)$  ⇔  $f'(x) > 0$ on $(a,b)$  (graph of $f'$ is above $x$-axis)
  • $f$ decreasing on $(a,b)$  ⇔  $f'(x) < 0$ on $(a,b)$  (graph of $f'$ is below $x$-axis)
  • $f$ has a local max or min at $x=c$  ⇔  $f'(c) = 0$  ($f'$ crosses the $x$-axis at $c$)
  • $f$ has a corner at $x=c$  ⇒  $f'(c)$ is undefined
  • Steeper slope of $f$  ⇒  $f'$ is farther from the $x$-axis
Common Mistake

✘ “$f'$ has the same zeros as $f$.”

This is false. The zeros of $f'$ are where $f$ has a horizontal tangent — i.e., a local max or min.

The zeros of $f$ (where $f$ crosses the $x$-axis) have nothing special to do with $f'$.

✔ Remember:  $f'(c) = 0$  ⇔  slope of $f$ is zero at $c$.

Quick checklist for sketching $f'$:
1. Mark every local max/min of $f$ → those become zeros of $f'$.
2. Shade where $f$ increases → $f'$ is positive there.
3. Shade where $f$ decreases → $f'$ is negative there.
4. Note any corners on $f$ → $f'$ is undefined there.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Downward parabola
Sketch $f'$ given $f(x) = -\tfrac{x^2}{4} + 1$.
$f'(x) = -\tfrac{x}{2}$ — a line with negative slope through the origin.
Check: $f$ has a local max at $x=0$ → $f'(0)=0$ ✔
For $x<0$: $f$ is increasing, so $f'>0$ ✔
For $x>0$: $f$ is decreasing, so $f'<0$ ✔

Left: $f$.   Right: $f$ (navy) with $f'$ (gold dashed).

Example 2  —  Cubic — two turning points
Sketch $f'$ given $f(x) = \tfrac{x^3-3x}{4}$.
$f'(x) = \tfrac{3x^2-3}{4} = \tfrac{3}{4}(x^2-1)$ — an upward parabola with zeros at $x=\pm 1$.
$f$ has a local max at $x=-1$ and local min at $x=1$, so $f'(-1)=f'(1)=0$ ✔
$f'<0$ between $x=-1$ and $x=1$ (where $f$ is decreasing) ✔
$f'>0$ outside $[-1,1]$ (where $f$ is increasing) ✔

Left: graph of $f$.   Right: graph of $f'$ (dashed gold).

Example 3  —  Corner function — f' undefined
Sketch $f'$ given $f(x) = |x|$.
For $x<0$: slope of $f$ is $-1$, so $f'(x)=-1$.
For $x>0$: slope of $f$ is $+1$, so $f'(x)=1$.
At $x=0$: left slope $\neq$ right slope  ⇒  $f'(0)$ is undefined (corner).

Left: $f(x)=|x|$ — a corner at $x=0$.   Right: $f'$ jumps from $-1$ to $1$; undefined at $x=0$.

Example 4  —  Reverse: graph of f′ given — describe f
The graph of $f'$ is shown. It is a line: $f'(x) = 2x - 2$. What does the graph of $f$ look like?
$f'(x) = 2x-2 = 0$ when $x=1$  ⇒  $f$ has a local extremum at $x=1$.
$f'<0$ for $x<1$  ⇒  $f$ is decreasing on $(-\infty,1)$.
$f'>0$ for $x>1$  ⇒  $f$ is increasing on $(1,\infty)$.
Since $f$ goes from decreasing to increasing, $x=1$ is a local minimum.
Note: we cannot say the exact value or shape of $f$ without integrating — many functions share this $f'$.
Example 5  —  Stretch — sketch both f′ and f″
For $f(x) = \tfrac{x^3-3x}{4}$: (a) sketch $f'$,  (b) sketch $f''$ from your $f'$ sketch.
(a) $f'(x)=\tfrac{3}{4}(x^2-1)$ — upward parabola, zeros at $x=\pm1$, min at $(0,-\tfrac{3}{4})$.
(b) $f''(x)=\tfrac{3}{4}(2x)=\tfrac{3x}{2}$ — a line through the origin, positive for $x>0$, negative for $x<0$, zero at $x=0$.
$f''=0$ at $x=0$ means $f'$ has a local min there  ⇒  $f$ has an inflection point at $x=0$.
Example 6  —  Stretch — match three graphs
Three graphs are shown below. One is $f$, one is $f'$, one is $f''$. Which is which?
A is $f$: cubic shape with local max and min.
B is $f'$: upward parabola, zeros at local max/min of A ($x=\pm1$).
C is $f''$: line through origin (derivative of B, which is a parabola).
Cross-check: $f''=0$ at $x=0$ ✔ — and B has its minimum at $x=0$ ✔
Practice Problems
1.
$f$ is decreasing on $(-3,1)$ and increasing on $(1,5)$. What is the sign of $f'$ on each interval, and what is $f'(1)$?
$f'<0$ on $(-3,1)$;  $f'>0$ on $(1,5)$;  $f'(1)=0$ (local min of $f$).
2.
$f(x)=x^2-4x$. Without computing $f'$, determine where $f'(x)=0$ from the graph.
$f(x)=x^2-4x=x(x-4)$ has vertex at $x=2$ (local min). So $f'(2)=0$.
3.
A function $f$ has a corner at $x=-2$. What can you say about $f'(-2)$?
$f'(-2)$ is undefined. The left and right slopes exist but differ.
4.
$f'(x)=x^2-4$. On what intervals is $f$ increasing? Where does $f$ have local extrema?
$f'>0$ when $x^2>4$, i.e. $x<-2$ or $x>2$. Local max at $x=-2$, local min at $x=2$.
5.
The graph of $f'$ passes through $(0,3)$ and $(2,0)$ (decreasing line). What does this tell you about $f$?
$f$ is increasing for $x<2$ (since $f'>0$), has a local max at $x=2$ ($f'=0$), and is decreasing for $x>2$ ($f'<0$).
6.
Stretch: $f$ has an inflection point at $x=4$. What does this tell you about $f'$ at $x=4$? About $f''$?
At an inflection point, $f'$ has a local max or min (changes from increasing to decreasing or vice versa). $f''(4)=0$.

Related Rates

Calculus I  ·  §3.2  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  STUDENT

A weather balloon is rising and its radius is growing as helium is pumped in. You know how fast the volume is changing. Can you figure out how fast the radius is changing, even though nobody ever wrote down radius as a function of time?
Setting Up a Related Rates Problem

Two or more quantities are related by an equation, and all of them are secretly functions of time $t$ — even if $t$ never appears in the equation itself.

  1. Draw a picture. Label every quantity that changes with a variable.
  2. Write an equation relating the variables, using only what's truly constant as a number.
  3. Differentiate implicitly with respect to $t$ — every variable gets a $\dfrac{d(\cdot)}{dt}$ via the chain rule.
  4. Plug in the known values (quantities and rates) at the instant in question, then solve for the missing rate.

Steps 2 and 3 are the whole trick: write the equation before you differentiate, and don't substitute numbers until after you differentiate.

Common Relating Equations
  • Right triangle (ladders, distances at right angles): $x^2+y^2=z^2$
  • Similar triangles (shadows, ramps): ratios of corresponding sides are equal
  • Circle: $A=\pi r^2$, $C=2\pi r$
  • Sphere: $V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, $S=4\pi r^2$
  • Cone: $V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2h$ (often with $r$ and $h$ proportional)
  • Cylinder: $V=\pi r^2h$
▸ Common Mistake

Students plug in the given numeric values before differentiating — substituting $y=4$ into $x^2+y^2=25$ to get $x=3$, then differentiating the now-constant equation $9+16=25$, which gives $0=0$. Useless.

Numbers describe one instant in time; the equation has to stay in terms of variables until after you differentiate, because $\dfrac{d}{dt}$ needs live variables to act on. Substitute the specific values only in the final, already-differentiated equation.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Basic implicit relation
If $xy=1$ and $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=3$, find $\dfrac{dx}{dt}$ when $x=1$.
At $x=1$: $y=1$ (since $xy=1$).
Differentiate $xy=1$: $x\dfrac{dy}{dt}+y\dfrac{dx}{dt}=0$
$(1)(3)+(1)\dfrac{dx}{dt}=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{dx}{dt}=-3$
Example 2  —  Sphere volume
The volume of a sphere is changing at a constant rate of $3\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is the radius growing when $r=4$?
$V=\dfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3\Rightarrow\dfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\dfrac{dr}{dt}$
$3=4\pi(16)\dfrac{dr}{dt}=64\pi\dfrac{dr}{dt}$
$\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{3}{64\pi}\text{ m/s}$
Example 3  —  Ladder sliding down a wall
A 5-foot ladder leans against a wall. The top slides down at $1\text{ ft/s}$. How fast is the base moving away from the wall when the top is $4$ ft off the ground?
$x^2+y^2=25$. At $y=4$: $x=3$.
Differentiate: $2x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=0$
With $\dfrac{dy}{dt}=-1$: $2(3)\dfrac{dx}{dt}+2(4)(-1)=0\Rightarrow\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dfrac{4}{3}\text{ ft/s}$
Example 4  —  Distance between two people
You and a friend start at the same spot. You walk north at $5$ mph; your friend walks west at $3$ mph. How fast is the distance between you changing $2$ hours later?
Let $x=$ friend's distance west, $y=$ your distance north, $z=$ distance apart: $x^2+y^2=z^2$.
At $t=2$: $x=6$, $y=10$, $z=\sqrt{136}=2\sqrt{34}$.
Differentiate: $x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=z\dfrac{dz}{dt}$
$6(3)+10(5)=2\sqrt{34}\cdot\dfrac{dz}{dt}\Rightarrow 68=2\sqrt{34}\cdot\dfrac{dz}{dt}$
$\dfrac{dz}{dt}=\sqrt{34}\approx5.83\text{ mph}$
Example 5  —  Lamp post: shadow length vs. shadow tip
A $5$-ft-tall person walks away from a $15$-ft lamp post at $4$ ft/s. Find (a) how fast the shadow's length is growing, and (b) how fast the tip of the shadow is moving away from the base of the lamp post.
Similar triangles: $s=\dfrac{h}{H-h}x=\dfrac{5}{10}x=\dfrac{x}{2}$
(a) $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{2}\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{2}(4)=2\text{ ft/s}$
(b) Tip's distance from the post is $x+s=x+\dfrac{x}{2}=\dfrac{3}{2}x$, so $\dfrac{d(\text{tip})}{dt}=\dfrac{3}{2}\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dfrac{3}{2}(4)=6\text{ ft/s}$ — faster than the shadow grows, since the person is moving too!
Practice Problems
1.
A 5-foot-tall person walks away from a 20-foot lamp post at $5$ ft/s. How fast is their shadow growing?
Similar triangles: $s=\dfrac{h}{H-h}x=\dfrac{5}{15}x=\dfrac{x}{3}$, so $\dfrac{ds}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{3}\dfrac{dx}{dt}=\dfrac{5}{3}\text{ ft/s}$
2.
A cylindrical tank of radius $5$ m is being filled at $3\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is the water level rising?
$V=\pi r^2h\Rightarrow\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\pi r^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$; $3=25\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dh}{dt}=\dfrac{3}{25\pi}\text{ m/s}$
3.
A circular ripple's radius grows at $2$ cm/s. How fast is the enclosed area growing when $r=10$ cm?
$A=\pi r^2\Rightarrow\dfrac{dA}{dt}=2\pi r\dfrac{dr}{dt}=2\pi(10)(2)=40\pi\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$
4.
Two cars leave an intersection, one heading east at $40$ mph, the other north at $30$ mph. How fast is the distance between them changing after 1 hour?
$x=40$, $y=30$, $z=\sqrt{1600+900}=50$. $x\dfrac{dx}{dt}+y\dfrac{dy}{dt}=z\dfrac{dz}{dt}\Rightarrow 40(40)+30(30)=50\dfrac{dz}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dz}{dt}=50\text{ mph}$
5.
Water drains from a conical tank (top radius $4$ m, height $10$ m) at $2\text{ m}^3/\text{min}$. How fast is the water level dropping when the depth is $5$ m?
Similar triangles give $r=\tfrac{2}{5}h$, so $V=\tfrac{4\pi}{75}h^3\Rightarrow\dfrac{dV}{dt}=\tfrac{4\pi}{25}h^2\dfrac{dh}{dt}$. At $h=5$: $-2=4\pi\dfrac{dh}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dh}{dt}=-\dfrac{1}{2\pi}\text{ m/min}$ (dropping)
6.
A spherical balloon is inflated at $100\text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$. How fast is the radius increasing when $r=5$ cm?
$\dfrac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\dfrac{dr}{dt}\Rightarrow 100=4\pi(25)\dfrac{dr}{dt}=100\pi\dfrac{dr}{dt}\Rightarrow\dfrac{dr}{dt}=\dfrac{1}{\pi}\text{ cm/s}$

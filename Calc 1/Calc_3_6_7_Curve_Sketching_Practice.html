Scenario — Particle Motion
A particle moves along a number line. Its position (in meters) at time $t$ seconds is:
Use the curve-sketching checklist to fully analyze and sketch $s(t)$.
Part (a) — Zeros & Intercept
Factor $s(t)$ completely. At what times in $[0,4]$ is the particle at position $s=0$? List all zeros (separate multiple answers with a comma).
Factor out $t$ first, then look at what remains as a perfect square.

$s(t)=t^3-6t^2+9t = t(t^2-6t+9) = t(t-3)^2$

Zeros at $t=0$ (simple zero) and $t=3$ (double zero — the curve touches but doesn't cross here).

$s(0)=0$: the particle starts at the origin. $s(3)=0$: the particle returns to the origin at $t=3$.

Differentiate $s(t)$, factor, and set equal to zero. Then use the First or Second Derivative Test to classify.

$s'(t)=3t^2-12t+9=3(t^2-4t+3)=3(t-1)(t-3)$

Critical times: $t=1$ and $t=3$.

$s''(t)=6t-12$.   $s''(1)=-6<0$ → local max at $t=1$: $s(1)=1-6+9=\mathbf{4}$ m.

$s''(3)=6>0$ → local min at $t=3$: $s(3)=27-54+27=\mathbf{0}$ m.

Physically: the particle moves forward to 4 m, reverses, returns to the origin, then moves forward again.

Set $s''(t)=0$ and verify the sign of $s''$ changes. What does the inflection point mean for the velocity?

$s''(t)=6t-12=0 \Rightarrow t=2$.   $s(2)=8-24+18=2$ m.

$s''<0$ on $(0,2)$ → concave down (velocity is decreasing).

$s''>0$ on $(2,4)$ → concave up (velocity is increasing).

Physical meaning: at $t=2$ the acceleration changes sign — this is when the deceleration is greatest as the particle slows to a stop, right before reversing direction at $t=3$.

Part (d) — Sketch
Check the sign of $s'(t)=3(t-1)(t-3)$ for a $t$ value between 1 and 3, say $t=2$.

$s'(2)=3(1)(-1)=-3<0$ → $s$ is decreasing on $(1,3)$. The particle is moving backward.

Complete picture of $s(t)$ on $[0,4]$:

• Starts at $s(0)=0$. • Rises (CU→CD) to local max $s=4$ at $t=1$.

• Falls through inflection $(2,\,2)$ where concavity changes (CD→CU).

• Touches zero again at $t=3$ (double zero — tangent is horizontal there).

• Rises to $s(4)=4$ at the right endpoint.

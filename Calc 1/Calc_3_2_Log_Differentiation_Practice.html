Logarithmic Differentiation

Calculus I  ·  §3.2  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
General Log Derivative $$\frac{d}{dx}[\log_b x] = \frac{1}{x \ln b}$$ Derived from change of base: $\log_b x = \dfrac{\ln x}{\ln b}$, so $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\log_b x] = \dfrac{1}{x \ln b}$. With chain rule: $\dfrac{d}{dx}[\log_b u] = \dfrac{u'}{u \ln b}$. When $b = e$: reduces to $\dfrac{1}{x}$ since $\ln e = 1$.
Logarithmic Differentiation For $y = f(x)$ with a variable exponent or messy product/quotient: (1) take $\ln$ of both sides, (2) use log rules to expand, (3) differentiate — left side gives $\dfrac{y'}{y}$, (4) solve $y' = y \cdot[\cdots]$, (5) substitute back. Example: $y = x^x \Rightarrow \ln y = x\ln x \Rightarrow \dfrac{y'}{y} = \ln x + 1 \Rightarrow y' = x^x(\ln x + 1)$.

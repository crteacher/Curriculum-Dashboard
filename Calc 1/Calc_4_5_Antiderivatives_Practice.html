Antiderivatives, Initial Values & Motion

Calculus I  ·  §4.5  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

Core Ideas
Going up the chain $a(t)\to v(t)+C_1\to s(t)+C_2$. Each integration adds a constant; the initial conditions $v(t_0)$ and $s(t_0)$ pin them down. Position is the antiderivative of velocity; velocity is the antiderivative of acceleration.
Initial Value Problems Given $F'(x)=f(x)$ and $F(a)=b$: antidifferentiate to get $F(x)=\cdots+C$, then substitute $F(a)=b$ to solve for $C$. Shortcut: if the condition is at $0$, all non-constant terms vanish and $C$ is the given value.
Displacement $\displaystyle\int_a^b v(t)\,dt=s(b)-s(a)$ is signed: it can be negative if the object moves back.

Factoring & Solving for Zeros: A Guided Walkthrough

Every critical-point problem ends in the same place: factor f′(x) and find where it equals 0. This walkthrough rebuilds that skill from the zero-product property up through cubics, quartics, fractional exponents and quotient-rule numerators, then finishes with a critical-point exam.

Work through the stages in order — each one builds on the last. In every stage: watch the short video if a concept feels shaky, try the “What went wrong?” checks (they are built around the exact mistakes students make), then work the practice problems — click New version for new numbers as many times as you want.
How to type your answers. Write exactly what you would write on paper, using ^ for powers and parentheses for brackets: 3x(x-2)^2, (2x+1)(x-3), x^2(x-4). Put fractional or negative exponents in parentheses: x^(1/2), 3x^(-1/2)(x-2). Separate zeros with commas: 0, -2, 5/2 (use fractions, not rounded decimals, unless the problem says to round). Any order and any equivalent arrangement is fine, as long as it is completely factored. The line under each box shows how your answer reads.

IRV & Plurality Comparison

Voting Theory  ·  Part 2.5  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

We now have two ways to count the same ballots. When plurality and IRV disagree, which one seems more fair — and can either one break a fairness criterion?
Review

Plurality Method: the choice with the most first-preference votes wins. Ties are possible and must be settled by some sort of run-off.

Most is not the same as majority. A majority is more than half of all votes.

Fairness criteria are statements that seem like they should be true in a fair election.

Condorcet Criterion: if one choice is preferred in every one-to-one comparison with the other choices, that choice (the Condorcet winner) should win the election.

Instant Runoff & Monotonicity

Instant Runoff Voting (IRV): the choice with the fewest first-place votes is eliminated and those ballots move to each voter’s next remaining choice. Repeat until a choice has a majority (over 50%).

Monotonicity Criterion: if voters change their ballots to increase the preference for a candidate, it should not harm that candidate’s chances of winning.

Teacher note: Group work: (1) Compare plurality and IRV on the Part 1.5 election. (2) Can plurality violate monotonicity? (10 min) (3) Can IRV violate Condorcet? (10 min, all-2nd-place example). Practice quiz time.

Example 2  —  Can IRV violate Condorcet?
Votes353431
1st choiceABC
2nd choiceCCA
3rd choiceBAB
Find the Condorcet winner, then run IRV.
A vs B: 66–34 (A wins)
A vs C: 35–65 (C wins)
B vs C: 34–66 (C wins)
Condorcet winner: C (but fewest first-place votes).
Round 1: A: 35, B: 34, C: 31 → eliminate C
Round 2: A: 66, B: 34 → A wins (majority of 100)
IRV eliminates the Condorcet winner first: yes, IRV can violate Condorcet.
Example 3  —  Plurality and monotonicity
Can plurality violate monotonicity?
No. Moving a candidate up on some ballots can only add to (never subtract from) that candidate’s first-place total and take votes from rivals, so plurality respects monotonicity. IRV does not (Part 2, Example 3).

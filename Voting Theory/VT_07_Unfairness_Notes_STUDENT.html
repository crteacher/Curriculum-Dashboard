(Un)fairness: Checking the Criteria

Voting Theory  ·  Part 5  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  STUDENT

We now have four fairness criteria and four voting methods. Can you find an election that “breaks” each method? Let’s go hunting.
The Four Criteria

Fairness criteria are statements that seem like they should be true in a fair election.

Condorcet Criterion: if one choice is preferred in every one-to-one comparison with the other choices, that choice (the Condorcet winner) should win the election.

Monotonicity Criterion: if voters change their ballots to increase the preference for a candidate, it should not harm that candidate’s chances of winning.

Majority Criterion: if a choice has a majority (more than everyone else put together) of the first-place votes, that choice should win.

Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (IIA): if a non-winning choice is added to or removed from the ballot, it should not change the winner.

How to Check an Election

Condorcet: find the Condorcet winner (if any). If your method’s winner is someone else, it violates. No Condorcet winner, or they agree → no violation.

Majority: does someone have a majority of first-place votes? Did they win? If not, it violates.

IIA: remove one or more non-winners. Does the winner change? Quick test: does someone beat the winner head to head?

Monotonicity: tricky — you will always be given the example to check.

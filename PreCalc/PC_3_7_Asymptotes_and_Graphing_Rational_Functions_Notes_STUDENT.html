Asymptotes & Graphing Rational Functions

Pre-Calculus  ·  §3.7  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  STUDENT

The function $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+1}{x}$ has $f(100)=100.01$ and $f(1000)=1000.001$. It never levels off like $\dfrac1x$ does. What line is the graph following, and how could you find it for $\dfrac{x^2+3x-4}{x-2}$?
Slant (Oblique) Asymptotes

Let $n=\deg(p)$, $d=\deg(q)$. A rational function has one of three end behaviors:

  • $n\le d$: horizontal asymptote (from §3.6)
  • $n=d+1$: slant asymptote $y=mx+k$
  • $n\ge d+2$: neither (the ends grow like a higher-degree polynomial)

To find the slant line, divide $p$ by $q$ (long or synthetic division). The quotient $mx+k$ is the asymptote; the remainder is ignored.

▸ Show Proof

Divide: $p(x)=q(x)\,(mx+k)+r(x)$ where $\deg(r)<\deg(q)$. Dividing both sides by $q(x)$:

$$f(x)=mx+k+\frac{r(x)}{q(x)}.$$

Behavior Beside a Vertical Asymptote

Near a vertical asymptote $x=a$, $f\to+\infty$ or $-\infty$. To decide which, find the sign of $f$ just left and just right of $a$:

  • Pick a test number close to $a$ on each side, or track the sign of each factor.
  • A factor $(x-a)$ to an odd power: the two sides go opposite ways.
  • A factor $(x-a)$ to an even power: both sides go the same way.
▸ Common Mistake

Students often draw a graph that goes up on both sides of every vertical asymptote, or switches sides at every one. The correct rule: check the exponent on that factor (odd flips, even does not) and the sign of everything else at $x=a$.

Another trap: a graph can cross a horizontal or slant asymptote in the middle. The asymptote only describes the far ends.

Graphing steps: factor and cancel (holes) → vertical asymptotes → end-behavior asymptote → intercepts → up/down on each side of each VA → connect.

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Slant asymptote by division
Find all asymptotes of $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2+3x-4}{x-2}$.
$x-2=0\Rightarrow$ VA at $x=2$ (the numerator is $2^2+6-4=6\neq0$). $\deg(p)=\deg(q)+1$, so divide: $\dfrac{x^2+3x-4}{x-2}=x+5+\dfrac{6}{x-2}$. Slant asymptote $y=x+5$. No HA.
Example 2 — Which end behavior?
For each, state whether there is a horizontal asymptote, a slant asymptote, or neither (and give it): (a) $\dfrac{2x^2+1}{x-1}$ (b) $\dfrac{x^2+5}{x^2-1}$ (c) $\dfrac{x^3+1}{x-1}$.
(a) $n=d+1$: divide, $2x+2+\dfrac{3}{x-1}$, slant $y=2x+2$. (b) $n=d$: HA $y=\dfrac11=1$. (c) $n=d+2$: neither.
Example 3 — Up or down beside each VA
For $g(x)=\dfrac{x-2}{(x+1)(x-3)}$, describe the behavior on each side of each vertical asymptote.
VAs at $x=-1$ and $x=3$, both odd powers, so each flips sides. At $x=-1$: just right, $(x-2)<0$, $(x+1)>0$, $(x-3)<0$ gives $\dfrac{-}{(+)(-)}=+$, so $\uparrow$ on the right and $\downarrow$ on the left. At $x=3$: just right, $\dfrac{+}{(+)(+)}=+$, so $\uparrow$ on the right, $\downarrow$ on the left. HA $y=0$.
Example 4 — Build the whole graph
Graph $f(x)=\dfrac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-1}$.
Zeros: $x=\pm2$. $f(0)=\dfrac{-4}{-1}=4$. VA $x=1$ (no cancellation). Divide: $\dfrac{x^2-4}{x-1}=x+1-\dfrac{3}{x-1}$, slant $y=x+1$. Beside the VA: just right of 1, $\dfrac{-}{+}=-$ so $\downarrow$; just left, $\uparrow$. On the far right the graph is below the slant line ($-\dfrac{3}{x-1}<0$); on the far left it is above.
Practice Problems
1.
Find the slant asymptote of $f(x)=\dfrac{2x^2-3x+5}{x-2}$.
$2x+1+\dfrac{7}{x-2}$, so $y=2x+1$.
2.
Does $g(x)=\dfrac{x^3-2x}{x^2+1}$ have a horizontal asymptote, slant asymptote, or neither?
$n=d+1$: slant. $\dfrac{x^3-2x}{x^2+1}=x-\dfrac{3x}{x^2+1}$, so $y=x$.
3.
Find the horizontal asymptote of $h(x)=\dfrac{4x^2+1}{2x^2-x}$.
$n=d$: $y=\dfrac42=2$.
4.
As $x\to2$, what does $f(x)=\dfrac{x+1}{(x-2)^2}$ do from the left and from the right?
Even power: both sides go the same way. Numerator $3>0$, denominator $>0$: $f\to+\infty$ from both sides.
5.
As $x\to-4^{+}$ and $x\to-4^{-}$, what does $f(x)=\dfrac{-3}{x+4}$ do?
Right side: $x+4>0$, so $f\to-\infty$. Left side: $x+4<0$, so $f\to+\infty$.
6.
List every asymptote and intercept of $f(x)=\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-3}$.
VA: $x=3$. Slant: $\dfrac{x^2-1}{x-3}=x+3+\dfrac{8}{x-3}$, $y=x+3$. x-intercepts: $\pm1$. y-intercept: $f(0)=\dfrac{-1}{-3}=\dfrac13$.

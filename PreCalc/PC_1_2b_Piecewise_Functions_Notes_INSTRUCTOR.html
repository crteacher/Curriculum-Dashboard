Piecewise Functions

Pre-Calculus  ·  §1.2 Pt 2  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

Cell phone plans often charge one rate for the first 500 minutes and a different rate after that. How would you write a single formula that captures this two-rate structure?
Defining Piecewise Functions

A piecewise function uses different formulas on different parts of its domain:

$$f(x)=\begin{cases} x^2 & x<0 \\ 2x+1 & x\geq 0 \end{cases}$$

To evaluate: determine which piece applies, then substitute.

$f(-3)=(-3)^2=9$   (use $x^2$ since $-3<0$)
$f(2)=2(2)+1=5$   (use $2x+1$ since $2\geq 0$)

▸ Common Mistake

At a boundary point, the inequality signs decide which piece to use — students grab the wrong branch. For $f(x)=\begin{cases}x^2 & x<0\\ 2x+1 & x\ge0\end{cases}$, finding $f(0)$ means using $2x+1$ (because $0\ge0$), giving $1$ — not $x^2=0$.

Only one piece owns the boundary: the one whose condition includes equality ($\le$ or $\ge$). Read the conditions before you pick a formula.

Domain and Range of Piecewise Functions

Read domain and range directly from the graph:

  • Domain — x-values the graph covers (left to right)
  • Range — y-values the graph reaches (bottom to top)

Domain: $[-3, 2]$
Range: $[0, 3]$

▸ Common Mistake

Swapping axes: reading the y-extent as the domain, or the x-extent as the range. Domain comes from the x-axis (horizontal); range comes from the y-axis (vertical).

Open circles exclude that endpoint — trace every piece to its endpoints before writing the final intervals.

Common types: absolute value, step functions (e.g. floor), tax-bracket models.

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Evaluate a piecewise function
Let $f(x)=\begin{cases}3x+1 & x<2\\x^2-1 & x\geq 2\end{cases}$. Find $f(-1)$, $f(2)$, $f(5)$.
$f(-1)=3(-1)+1=-2$; $f(2)=4-1=3$; $f(5)=25-1=24$.
Example 2 — Domain and range from a graph
The graph of $g$ is shown. State the domain and range.
Domain: $[-3, 3]$. Range: $[-1, 2]$.
Example 3 — Absolute value piecewise
Write $f(x)=|2x-4|$ as a piecewise function and evaluate $f(1)$ and $f(3)$.
$f(x)=\begin{cases}4-2x & x<2\\2x-4 & x\geq 2\end{cases}$. $f(1)=2$; $f(3)=2$.
Example 4 — Real-world piecewise
A parking garage charges $\$3$ for the first hour and $\$1.50$ per hour after that. Write $C(h)$ for $h\geq 0$ hours.
$C(h)=\begin{cases}3 & 0\leq h\leq 1\\3+1.5(h-1) & h>1\end{cases}$. E.g. $C(3)=3+3=\$6$.

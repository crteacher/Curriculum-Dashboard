Quadratics and Parametric Equations

Pre-Calculus  ·  §3.3  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

A ball is thrown at an angle — its $x$ and $y$ positions each depend on time separately. How can we write two equations in $t$ and then eliminate $t$ to get the path?
Quadratic Applications

Projectile height: $h(t)=-16t^2+v_0 t+h_0$ (in feet)
or $h(t)=-4.9t^2+v_0 t+h_0$ (in meters).

Maximum height: at vertex $t=-\dfrac{v_0}{2(-16)}=\dfrac{v_0}{32}$.

Time to hit ground: set $h(t)=0$, solve via quadratic formula.

▸ Show Proof

Why does the maximum height happen at $t=\dfrac{v_0}{32}$? The graph of $h(t)=-16t^2+v_0t+h_0$ is a parabola opening downward (the $t^2$ coefficient is negative), so its single highest point is the vertex. We just need to locate that vertex.

Complete the square on the general quadratic $at^2+bt+c$. Factor $a$ out of the first two terms, then fold them into a perfect square:

$$at^2+bt+c = a\left(t+\tfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \frac{b^2}{4a} + c.$$

Only the squared part depends on $t$, and a square is never negative — it is smallest (equal to $0$) exactly when its inside is $0$, i.e. when $t=-\dfrac{b}{2a}$. So the vertex always sits at $t=-\dfrac{b}{2a}$.

For projectile height, $a=-16$ and $b=v_0$, giving

$$t=-\frac{v_0}{2(-16)}=\frac{v_0}{32}.$$

∎

Setup: identify $a$, $b$, $c$ from the problem context. The coefficient $a$ is always negative for projectile motion (gravity acts downward).

Parametric Equations

Parametric equations give $x$ and $y$ each as a function of a parameter $t$:

$x = f(t), \quad y = g(t)$

Pick a value of $t$, compute $(x,y)$, and mark the point. As $t$ increases the point traces a path through the plane.

Example: $x(t)=2t,\; y(t)=t^2$

Eliminating the parameter: solve one equation for $t$ and substitute into the other. Here $t=\tfrac{x}{2}$, so $y=\bigl(\tfrac{x}{2}\bigr)^2=\tfrac{x^2}{4}$. You recover the curve shape — but $t$ (and any timing it encodes) is gone.

Cannon Problem — Full Walkthrough

Setup. A cannon fires a ball from ground level. It leaves with horizontal speed 80 ft/s and vertical speed 96 ft/s. Write the parametric equations, then answer each question below.

Why two separate equations? Horizontal motion has no force acting on it — the ball coasts at 80 ft/s forever. Vertical motion fights gravity the whole way up, slows to a stop, then falls back down. These are completely different stories, so we write them separately:

$x(t)=80t \qquad y(t)=-16t^2+96t$

Step 1 — Find position at $t=2$ s
Substitute $t=2$ into both equations. This is what parametrics let you do that $y=f(x)$ cannot — pinpoint WHERE the ball is at a specific moment.
$x(2)=80(2)=160$ ft from the cannon.
$y(2)=-16(4)+96(2)=-64+192=128$ ft high.
Step 2 — Maximum height
$y(t)=-16t^2+96t$ is a downward parabola. Its vertex gives the highest point. Use $t^*=\dfrac{v_0}{32}$.
$t^*=\dfrac{96}{32}=3$ s.
$y(3)=-16(9)+96(3)=-144+288=\mathbf{144}$ ft.
Step 3 — Landing time & range
The ball lands when $y(t)=0$ again (with $t>0$). Then use $x(t)$ to find how far it traveled.
$-16t^2+96t=0\;\Rightarrow\;-16t(t-6)=0\;\Rightarrow\;t=6$ s.
Range: $x(6)=80(6)=\mathbf{480}$ ft.

Step 4 — Eliminate the parameter to get the path equation $y = f(x)$:

$t = \dfrac{x}{80} \;\Rightarrow\; y = -16\!\left(\dfrac{x}{80}\right)^{\!2}+96\!\cdot\!\dfrac{x}{80} = -\dfrac{x^2}{400}+\dfrac{6x}{5}$

This tells you the shape of the arc — a parabola. But notice: you can no longer say "where is it at $t=2$?" The timing is gone. That is why the parametric form is more powerful for motion problems: always keep the $t$-equations until you have answered every time-based question first.

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Ball height application
A ball is thrown upward from 5 ft at 64 ft/s. Find max height and when it lands.
$h(t)=-16t^2+64t+5$. Max at $t=2$: $h=69$ ft. Lands when $h=0$: $t=\dfrac{-64\pm\sqrt{4096+320}}{-32}\approx 4.08$ s.
Example 2 — Revenue optimization
Revenue is $R(x)=-x^2+120x$ where $x$ is units sold. Find max revenue.
Vertex at $x=60$; $R(60)=-3600+7200=\$3600$.
Example 3 — Eliminate parameter
Eliminate $t$ from $x=t+1$, $y=t^2-2$.
$t=x-1$; $y=(x-1)^2-2=x^2-2x-1$.
Example 4 — Projectile path
A ball: $x=30t$, $y=-16t^2+20t+4$. Eliminate $t$ to get the path.
$t=\dfrac{x}{30}$; $y=-16\left(\dfrac{x}{30}\right)^2+\dfrac{20x}{30}+4=-\dfrac{x^2}{56.25}+\dfrac{2x}{3}+4$.
Practice Problems
1.
A ball is thrown from 3 ft at 48 ft/s. Write $h(t)$ and find max height.
$h(t)=-16t^2+48t+3$; max at $t=1.5$: $h=39$ ft.
2.
Revenue: $R(p)=-5p^2+300p$. Find price $p$ for max revenue.
$p=30$; max $R=\$4500$.
3.
Eliminate $t$: $x=3t-1$, $y=2t+4$.
$t=\dfrac{x+1}{3}$; $y=\dfrac{2(x+1)}{3}+4=\dfrac{2x+14}{3}$.
4.
For $x=\cos t$, $y=\sin t$, what curve is traced?
$x^2+y^2=1$, a unit circle.
5.
A profit model: $P(x)=-2x^2+80x-400$. Find break-even points.
$-2x^2+80x-400=0\Rightarrow x^2-40x+200=0\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{40\pm\sqrt{800}}{2}=20\pm 10\sqrt{2}\approx 5.9,34.1$.
6.
State the vertex of $h(t)=-4.9t^2+14t+1$ and interpret it.
Vertex at $t=\dfrac{14}{9.8}\approx1.43$ s; $h\approx 11$ m — maximum height of the projectile.

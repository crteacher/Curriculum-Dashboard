§3.2c — Quadratics: Putting It All Together

PreCalc  ·  §3.2c  ·  Instructor: Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026

You have a quadratic in standard form. Before converting, ask: what do I actually need to know? What does each form let you read off without any calculation?

▸ Think about it, then reveal

Standard form gives the $y$-intercept instantly ($c$). Vertex form gives the max or min height. Factored form gives where the function is zero. Convert to the form that answers your question.

Quick Reference — The Three Forms

FormEquationWhat you can read directly
Standard$ax^2+bx+c$y-intercept: $(0,\ c)$
Vertex x-coord: $x = -\dfrac{b}{2a}$  (then plug in for y)
Vertex$a(x-h)^2+k$Vertex: $(h,\ k)$;  axis: $x=h$;  max/min value: $k$
Factored$a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$Zeros: $x=r_1$ and $x=r_2$;  axis: $x=\dfrac{r_1+r_2}{2}$

The value $a$ is the same in all three forms — it controls direction (up if $a>0$, down if $a<0$). Quadratic formula (zeros from standard form): $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$

Core Ideas

Converting between forms

Solid arrows = technique to learn  ·  Gray dashed = expand to reverse

What each form tells you directly

Standard   $ax^2+bx+c$

$\bullet$ $y$-intercept: the point $(0,\,c)$

Vertex   $a(x-h)^2+k$

$\bullet$ Vertex at $(h,\,k)$; axis of symmetry $x=h$

$\bullet$ Max value $k$ (if $a<0$) or min value $k$ (if $a>0$)

Factored   $a(x-r_1)(x-r_2)$

$\bullet$ Zeros (roots): $x=r_1$ and $x=r_2$

$\bullet$ Axis of symmetry: $x=\dfrac{r_1+r_2}{2}$ (midpoint of roots)

The value $a$ is the same in all three forms — it controls direction: opens up if $a>0$, down if $a<0$.

Worked Examples
Example 1  —  Cannonball — all three forms

A cannonball is fired from a 10-foot platform at 64 ft/s. Its height (in feet) after $t$ seconds is$$h(t) = -16t^2 + 64t + 10$$

(a) What is the launch height?

(b) When does the cannonball reach its maximum height, and how high does it go?

(c) When does the cannonball hit the ground?

(a) Use standard form — read $c$ directly.
$h(0) = 10$.  Launch height: 10 ft.

(b) Complete the square to get vertex form.
$h(t)=-16(t^2-4t)+10=-16(t^2-4t+4-4)+10=-16(t-2)^2+64+10$
$h(t)=-16(t-2)^2+74$
Vertex $(2,74)$  →  max height 74 ft at $t=2$ s.

(c) Set $h=0$; won't factor — use the quadratic formula.
$8t^2-32t-5=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t=\dfrac{32\pm\sqrt{1024+160}}{16}=\dfrac{32\pm 4\sqrt{74}}{16}=\dfrac{8\pm\sqrt{74}}{4}$
Positive root: $t=\dfrac{8+\sqrt{74}}{4}\approx 4.15$ s.

Example 2  —  Complete the square

Write $f(x) = x^2 - 6x + 5$ in vertex form. State the vertex and axis of symmetry.

$f(x)=(x^2-6x+9)-9+5=(x-3)^2-4$

Vertex $(3,-4)$, axis $x=3$, opens up.

Example 3  —  Factor (nice discriminant)

Find the zeros of $f(x) = x^2 - 5x + 6$.

Check: does $b^2-4ac$ give a perfect square?

$b^2-4ac = 25-24=1$  ✓  factors nicely.

$(x-2)(x-3)=0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x=2 \text{ and } x=3$

Axis: $x=\frac{2+3}{2}=2.5$

Example 4  —  Quadratic formula (won't factor)

Find the zeros of $g(x) = x^2 - 3x - 1$.

Check the discriminant first.

$b^2-4ac=9+4=13$ — not a perfect square, so factor won't work.

$x=\dfrac{3\pm\sqrt{13}}{2}$

$x\approx 3.30$ or $x\approx -0.30$

Example 5  —  Build from given information

A parabola has vertex $(-1,\,9)$ and passes through $(2,\,0)$. Write it in vertex form, then expand to standard form.

Vertex form: $a(x+1)^2+9$.

Plug in $(2,0)$: $a(9)+9=0 \Rightarrow a=-1$.

Vertex form: $-(x+1)^2+9$

Standard: $-(x^2+2x+1)+9 = -x^2-2x+8$

Practice Problem

Practice Problem — A model rocket is launched from a 6-foot stand. Its height in feet after $t$ seconds is$$h(t) = -16t^2 + 48t + 6$$

Use this function for all four parts below.

(a)
What is the launch height? Which form makes this a one-step read?

Read $c$ from standard form: $h(0)=6$. Launch height: 6 ft.

(b)
When does the rocket reach its maximum height, and how high does it go? Show the completing-the-square steps.

$h(t)=-16(t^2-3t)+6=-16\!\left(t-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^2+36+6=-16\!\left(t-\tfrac{3}{2}\right)^2+42$
Vertex $\left(\tfrac{3}{2},\,42\right)$  →  max height 42 ft at $t=1.5$ s.

(c)
When does the rocket hit the ground? Try to factor first, then use the formula if needed.

$b^2-4ac=48^2+4(16)(6)=2304+384=2688$ — not a perfect square.

$8t^2-24t-3=0 \Rightarrow t=\dfrac{24\pm\sqrt{672}}{16}=\dfrac{6\pm\sqrt{42}}{4}$

Positive root: $t=\dfrac{6+\sqrt{42}}{4}\approx 3.12$ s.

(d)
What is the axis of symmetry? Give two ways to see it.

From vertex form: axis $x=\tfrac{3}{2}$.

Also: midpoint of the two roots $= \dfrac{\frac{6-\sqrt{42}}{4}+\frac{6+\sqrt{42}}{4}}{2}=\dfrac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{1}=\tfrac{3}{2}$.  $t=1.5$ s.

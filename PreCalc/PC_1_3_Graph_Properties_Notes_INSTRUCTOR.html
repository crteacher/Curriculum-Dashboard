Graph Properties

Pre-Calculus  ·  §1.3  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  INSTRUCTOR

If you graph your distance from home over a day, what does it mean when the graph goes up? Down? Stays flat? How do the highest and lowest points relate to your travel?
Increasing, Decreasing, Constant

Reading graphs left to right:

  • Increasing on $(a,b)$: $a<x_1<x_2<b\Rightarrow f(x_1)<f(x_2)$
  • Decreasing on $(a,b)$: $a<x_1<x_2<b\Rightarrow f(x_1)>f(x_2)$
  • Constant on $(a,b)$: $f(x_1)=f(x_2)$ for all $x_1,x_2$

Report intervals using $x$-values, using open intervals.

▸ Common Mistake

Increasing and decreasing intervals are reported with $x$-values, not $y$-values. Students see the curve rise from height $2$ to height $5$ and write "increasing on $(2,5)$" — but those are outputs.

The interval names the $x$-positions over which the curve rises. Read the horizontal axis, and use open intervals at the turning points.

Positive & Negative Intervals

A graph is positive on $(a,b)$ where it lies above the $x$-axis: $f(x)>0$. It's negative where it lies below: $f(x)<0$. It equals zero at each $x$-intercept — the boundary between the two.

$f(x)=x^2-4$: negative between the roots, positive outside.
▸ Common Mistake

Positive/negative describes the $y$-value (above or below the $x$-axis); increasing/decreasing describes the trend (rising or falling left to right). These aren't the same thing — a graph can be negative and increasing at the same time.

For example, $f(x)=x^2-4$ on $(0,2)$ is negative (it stays below the axis there) but increasing (it's climbing back up toward zero as $x$ increases). Don't assume "negative" means "decreasing."

Local & Absolute Max/Min

Local max/min: highest/lowest in some open interval around the point.

Absolute max/min: highest/lowest on the entire domain (may not exist).

Average Rate of Change

Average rate of change from $a$ to $b$:

$$\text{ARC}=\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$$

This equals the slope of the secant line connecting $(a,f(a))$ and $(b,f(b))$.

▸ Common Mistake

Average rate of change keeps the same order on top and bottom: $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}$. Students flip one of them — writing $\dfrac{f(b)-f(a)}{a-b}$ — which reverses the sign, or they invert the whole thing to $\dfrac{b-a}{f(b)-f(a)}$.

It's a slope: change in output over change in input, with both differences taken in the same direction ($b$ then $a$).

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Positive/negative & increasing/decreasing from a graph
For the graph of $f(x)=x^3-3x$ shown below, state where $f$ is positive/negative and where it is increasing/decreasing.
Positive on $(-\sqrt3,0)\cup(\sqrt3,\infty)$; negative on $(-\infty,-\sqrt3)\cup(0,\sqrt3)$. Increasing on $(-\infty,-1)$ and $(1,\infty)$; decreasing on $(-1,1)$.
Example 2 — Positive/negative & increasing/decreasing from a graph
For the graph of $f(x)=-x^2-2x+3$ shown below, state where $f$ is positive/negative and where it is increasing/decreasing.
Positive on $(-3,1)$; negative on $(-\infty,-3)\cup(1,\infty)$. Increasing on $(-\infty,-1)$; decreasing on $(-1,\infty)$.
Example 3 — Local vs. absolute extrema from a graph
The graph of $f(x)=x^4-4x^2$ is shown below. Identify the local maximum and the absolute minimum.
Local max at $(0,0)$ — but not the absolute max, since the curve rises without bound. Absolute min of $-4$, attained at both $x=-\sqrt2$ and $x=\sqrt2$.
Example 4 — Average rate of change from a graph
For $f(x)=x^2$ shown below, find the average rate of change from the marked point $(0,0)$ to the marked point $(3,9)$.
$\dfrac{9-0}{3-0}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3$ — the slope of the dashed secant line.
Practice Problems
1.
A graph increases on $(-2,1)$, has a local max of 5 at $x=1$, then decreases. Describe the function's behavior.
Increasing on $(-2,1)$, local max $=5$ at $x=1$, decreasing after.
2.
Find the ARC of $f(x)=x^2+1$ from $x=0$ to $x=4$.
$\dfrac{17-1}{4}=4$.
3.
Find the ARC of $f(x)=2x-3$ from $x=1$ to $x=5$.
$\dfrac{7-(-1)}{4}=2$ (equals slope of line).
4.
A function has an absolute min of $-2$ but no absolute max. Sketch a possible graph.
E.g. $y=x^2-2$, opening upward with vertex at minimum.
5.
On what interval(s) is $f(x)=|x|$ decreasing?
Decreasing on $(-\infty,0)$.
6.
Find the ARC of $g(x)=x^3$ from $x=-1$ to $x=2$.
$\dfrac{8-(-1)}{3}=3$.

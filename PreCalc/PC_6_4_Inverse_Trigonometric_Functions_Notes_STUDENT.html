Inverse Trigonometric Functions

Pre-Calculus  ·  §6.4  ·  Christopher Halverson  ·  Fall 2026  ·  STUDENT

If $\sin\theta=0.5$, you know $\theta=30°$ — but the sine function repeats. How do we define a unique "arcsin" that gives back exactly one answer?
Defining the Inverse Trig Functions

Restrict domains so the trig function is 1-1, then define the inverse:

$y=\arcsin x$: domain $[-1,1]$, range $\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2},\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$
$y=\arccos x$: domain $[-1,1]$, range $[0,\pi]$
$y=\arctan x$: domain $(-\infty,\infty)$, range $\left(-\dfrac{\pi}{2},\dfrac{\pi}{2}\right)$

$\arcsin$ outputs angles in QI or QIV (first and fourth quadrants).
$\arccos$ outputs angles in QI or QII.

Evaluating Inverse Trig

$\arcsin(\sin\theta)=\theta$ only if $\theta\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$.
$\sin(\arcsin x)=x$ for $x\in[-1,1]$.

Composition: simplify using a right triangle. To find $\cos(\arcsin\frac{3}{5})$: draw right triangle with opp $=3$, hyp $=5$; adj $=4$; answer $=\frac{4}{5}$.

▸ Show Proof

Why does the right-triangle trick always work? Let $\theta=\arcsin x$ for some $x\in[-1,1]$. By definition this means $\sin\theta=x$, and since $\arcsin$ only outputs angles in $\left[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right]$, we know $\cos\theta\ge 0$.

Now use the Pythagorean identity, which holds for every angle: $\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1$.

Substitute $\sin\theta=x$: $\quad x^2+\cos^2\theta=1$, so $\cos^2\theta=1-x^2$.

Since $\cos\theta\ge 0$ here, taking the square root gives $$\cos(\arcsin x)=\sqrt{1-x^2}.$$

The triangle picture is just a shortcut for this same computation: if $\sin\theta=\frac{opp}{hyp}=x$, the Pythagorean theorem says $adj=\sqrt{hyp^2-opp^2}$, and $\cos\theta=\frac{adj}{hyp}$ reproduces exactly the formula above. That's why building a triangle with "opposite $=x$, hypotenuse $=1$" (or any equivalent scaled version, like opp $=3$, hyp $=5$) and reading off the missing side always gives the right answer.

∎

Restricted Domains — The Graphs

▬ restricted sin, cos, tan    ▬ inverse    - - - y = x (the mirror line)

Each inverse is the reflection across $y=x$ of a trig function cut down to one 1-1 piece: $\sin x$ on $[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]$, $\cos x$ on $[0,\pi]$, $\tan x$ on $\left(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}\right)$. The dots mark endpoints that swap $x$ and $y$; $\arctan$ has horizontal asymptotes $y=\pm\frac{\pi}{2}$ where $\tan$ has vertical ones.

Worked Examples
Example 1 — Evaluate arcsin, arccos, arctan
Find: (a) $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt2}{2}$ (b) $\arccos(-1)$ (c) $\arctan(-1)$.
(a) $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$. (b) $\pi$. (c) $-\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
Example 2 — Composition
Simplify $\cos\left(\arcsin\dfrac{5}{13}\right)$.
Triangle: opp 5, hyp 13, adj 12. $\cos=\dfrac{12}{13}$.
Example 3 — Simplify
Simplify $\sin\left(\arctan\dfrac{3}{4}\right)$.
Triangle: opp 3, adj 4, hyp 5. $\sin=\dfrac{3}{5}$.
Example 4 — Inverse with outside range
Find $\arcsin\left(\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6}\right)$.
$\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ not in $[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$. $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6}=-\dfrac{1}{2}$. $\arcsin(-\dfrac{1}{2})=-\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.
